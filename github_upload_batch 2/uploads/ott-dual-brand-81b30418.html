One app, two content worlds — designing a dual-brand streaming experience.

A global movie and drama streaming platform wanted to launch a second content library — Indian films and dramas — inside its existing app, without it feeling bolted on, and without splitting into two products.

ENGAGEMENTFreelance · 2 months
PLATFORMMobile app · iOS & Android
MY ROLEResearch → UI → dev handoff
01 — Context

Two content libraries, one subscription.

The client runs an international movie and drama streaming app. They were adding a second content vertical focused on Indian films and dramas, sold under the same subscription — not as a separate app or a separate purchase.

I came on as the sole designer for this, covering research, UX, UI, a small supporting design system, and handoff to the client's dev team.

02 — Users & role

Two audiences, one account.

Existing subscribers

Came to the app for its original catalogue. Needed the core experience to stay familiar — the new content shouldn't crowd their home feed or feel like a different app took over.

New Indian-content audience

Drawn specifically to the Indian dramas and films. Needed to feel like this library had its own identity and curation — not a folder buried inside someone else's app.

ResearchUX flows / IAUI designDesign systemPrototypingDev handoffClient management
03 — The constraint

Two brands, without two apps.

The two libraries needed to read as genuinely different experiences — different tone, different curation, different visual identity — while sharing one account, one subscription, and one navigation. Splitting into two apps would have solved the identity problem and created a worse one: duplicated logins, duplicated billing, and two products to maintain.

04 — Approach

One shared shell, two theme layers.

I designed a single app shell — account, subscription, and navigation shared across both libraries — with two theme layers sitting on top, one per content world. Each theme changes the accent colour, imagery, and curation rhythm of the discovery feed and content pages, while the underlying structure stays identical.

A switcher on the home feed lets people move between the two worlds without it feeling like a hard split — the two catalogues read as siblings under one platform, not two products stitched together at login.
